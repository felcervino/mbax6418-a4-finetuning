# S10 Report

**What I compared.** Three ways of labeling 300 Amazon Gift Cards reviews (100 per class) as POSITIVE, NEUTRAL or NEGATIVE, using star ratings as the answer key (4 or 5 stars positive, 3 neutral, 1 or 2 negative). Two use a large hosted model, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (35 billion parameters, about 3 billion active at a time): once with no examples and once shown 6 examples. The third is a small model I trained, Gemma 3 4B (4,329,881,968 parameters, of which 29,802,496 were adjusted through a small add-on called an adapter, on a compressed 4-bit copy, using Unsloth). Training ran on a Tesla T4 GPU, tested directly, for 114 steps over 2 passes through 900 training reviews, and took 678.8 seconds (about 11 minutes).

**Why these choices.** The small model had to fit a T4; Gemma 3 4B used 5.15 GB during training. Settings start from Unsloth's documented example (rank 16, learning rate 0.0002, seed 3407); the 16 reviews per step and the 2 passes were my choices, because the documentation is not consistent on them. The 6 examples are training reviews, the first 2 per class from a shortlist built by a fixed rule (150 to 450 characters, no "Five Stars" titles). Two are noisy: reviews 13547 and 9272 have star ratings that do not match their text.

**Results** (300 test reviews, no invalid answers). Balanced accuracy is the average share of each class found correctly; macro-F1 also penalizes wrong guesses.

| Approach | Balanced accuracy | Macro-F1 | Correct / incorrect | NEUTRAL recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hosted, no examples | 0.747 | 0.717 | 224 / 76 | 0.35 |
| Hosted, 6 examples | 0.747 | 0.718 | 224 / 76 | 0.35 |
| Fine-tuned Gemma 3 4B | 0.810 | 0.805 | 243 / 57 | 0.63 |

**Best approach, and where it fails.** The fine-tuned model scored highest, mostly on NEUTRAL reviews: the hosted model called 50 (no examples) or 51 (6 examples) of 100 neutral reviews negative, the fine-tuned model 23, for example review 30030 ("Ok but I won't do it again", 3 stars). Its weakness is the other direction: it called 11 of 100 negative reviews neutral, against 1 for the hosted model with no examples, for example review 30137 ("Not Amazons Usual Excellence", 1 star). My reading: training on 300 neutral reviews taught the small model where NEUTRAL sits, while the hosted model leans negative on mixed reviews; the price is that the small model over-guesses NEUTRAL.

**A caution about titles.** 50 reviews have titles like "Five Stars", which give the rating away. The fine-tuned model got all 50 right (the hosted runs 39 and 42). On the other 250 reviews its lead shrinks: 193 correct, against 185 and 182.

**What the statistics support.** A McNemar test counts only the reviews where exactly one of two approaches is right. The fine-tuned model was right alone on 34 reviews against 15 for the hosted model with no examples (p = 0.0094), and on 31 against 12 versus 6 examples (p = 0.0054). The p-value is how likely so lopsided a split would be if both approaches were equally accurate; it does not measure how big or useful the difference is. The two hosted runs split 6 and 6 (p = 1.0), so the examples left no net difference. A follow-up calculation outside this notebook, without the 50 star-word reviews, gave p = 0.26 and p = 0.09. My reading: the advantage is real on these 300 reviews but fragile, and I would not trust it to carry over to new reviews.

**Limits.** The ones I weigh most: I ran each approach once, so no number has a spread; star ratings are a noisy answer key, especially at 3 stars; and the hosted model is far larger, so this is not a like-for-like comparison.

**Is the gain worth the cost?** Fine-tuning took 678.8 seconds on one T4, produced a 110 MB adapter, and needed 900 labeled reviews. Labeling the 300 test reviews then took 141.6 seconds on that T4 using 6.23 GB, against 30.4 seconds for 300 few-shot requests to the hosted model, which needs a remote service but no training. The fine-tuned model scored 0.063 higher in balanced accuracy, but much of that lead is in the star-word titles. My answer: for a one-off job of 300 reviews, no. Fine-tuning would be worth it if reviews had to be labeled again and again on a small GPU without a remote service, and if the gain held up on a fresh sample.

**A decision I investigated with my agent.** My first small model, Phi-4-mini, trained only 2 of the model's 4 kinds of layers in a trial run, and a retest gave the same result. It offered three options (keep the weaker adapter, work around Unsloth, switch models) and recommended Gemma 3 4B as the only option that kept the full adapter inside Unsloth's supported path. I mostly trusted its recommendation and chose the switch without independently checking its evidence. What protects the result is what came after: a new check in the notebook, which fails unless all 7 kinds of layers are trained, passed with Gemma 3 4B (238 trained modules), and the reloaded saved adapter reproduced its predictions. The model the adapter was trained on, and must be loaded with, is unsloth/gemma-3-4b-it-unsloth-bnb-4bit, Unsloth's 4-bit build of google/gemma-3-4b-it, and adapter_config.json names exactly that id.
