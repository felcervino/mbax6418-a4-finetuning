# Report

**What I compared.** Three ways of labeling 300 Amazon Gift Cards reviews (100 per class) as POSITIVE, NEUTRAL or NEGATIVE, with star ratings as the reference (4 or 5 stars positive, 3 neutral, 1 or 2 negative): a large hosted model with no examples, the same model shown 6 examples, and a small model I fine-tuned. The hosted model is Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (35 billion parameters, about 3 billion active at a time). The small model is Gemma 3 4B (4,329,881,968 parameters, of which 29,802,496 were trained as a small add-on called a LoRA adapter, on a compressed 4-bit copy, using Unsloth). Training ran on a Tesla T4 GPU, tested directly, for 114 steps over 2 passes through 900 training reviews and took 678.8 seconds (about 11 minutes).

**Why these choices.** The small model had to fit a T4; Gemma 3 4B used 5.15 GB during training. Phi-4-mini was my first pick but could not be fully trained with Unsloth (last paragraph). Training settings come from Unsloth's documentation (rank 16, learning rate 0.0002, 8-bit AdamW, 16 reviews per step, seed 3407, scored only on the model's answer). The 6 examples are training reviews, the first 2 per class from a shortlist built by a fixed rule (text of 150 to 450 characters, no "Five Stars" titles); I approved them. Two are noisy: review 13547 has 4 stars but describes a confusing reload, and 9272 has 1 star but reads like a neutral question. None is a test review.

**Results** (300 test reviews; no invalid answers). Balanced accuracy averages the share of reviews found in each class; macro-F1 is a similar average that also penalizes wrong guesses.

| Approach | Balanced accuracy | Macro-F1 | Correct / incorrect | NEUTRAL recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hosted, zero-shot | 0.747 | 0.717 | 224 / 76 | 0.35 |
| Hosted, 6 examples | 0.747 | 0.718 | 224 / 76 | 0.35 |
| Fine-tuned Gemma 3 4B | 0.810 | 0.805 | 243 / 57 | 0.63 |

**Best approach and where it fails.** The fine-tuned model scored highest, mainly on NEUTRAL reviews: the hosted model labeled 50 (zero-shot) or 51 (6 examples) of 100 neutral reviews negative, the fine-tuned model 23. Review 30030 ("Ok but I won't do it again", a mixed 3-star review) was called negative by both hosted runs and neutral by the fine-tuned model. Its weakness is the other direction: it called 11 of 100 negative reviews neutral, against 1 for hosted zero-shot, as in review 30137 ("Not Amazons Usual Excellence", a 1-star complaint). Some errors are shared: review 30008 (a 3-star pop-up box complaint) was called negative by all three, and review 30011 (title and text both "Gift", 5 stars) neutral by all three.

**Titles that give the rating away.** 50 reviews have titles like "Five Stars". The fine-tuned model got all 50 right (the hosted runs 39 and 42). A check I ran afterwards, outside the notebook (evidence/star_title_sensitivity.txt), shows much of its lead sits there: on the other 250 reviews it has 193 correct against 185 (zero-shot) and 182 (6 examples). Those 50 are mostly positive (29 positive, 18 neutral, 3 negative).

**What the statistics support.** An exact McNemar test looks only at reviews where two approaches disagree. Over all 300: fine-tuned versus zero-shot, 34 right only for the fine-tuned model and 15 only for zero-shot (p = 0.0094); versus 6 examples, 31 and 12 (p = 0.0054); zero-shot versus 6 examples, 6 and 6 (p = 1.0), so the examples changed nothing measurable. Three tests, no adjustment. A small p-value says the disagreements lean one way on these 300 reviews, nothing more, and without the star-word titles the comparisons are no longer clear (p = 0.26 and p = 0.09). Limits: one dataset, one small model, one set of 6 examples, one run per approach; the hosted model is far larger; star ratings are noisy, especially 3 stars. Repeated hosted calls on 30 training reviews changed label for 1 review (zero-shot) and none (few-shot). In the final run 238 of 300 hosted zero-shot answers, and 2 of 300 few-shot, were reused from an earlier attempt that stopped on a file error.

**Do the gains justify the cost?** Fine-tuning took 678.8 seconds on one T4, a 110 MB adapter, and 900 labeled reviews. Answering 300 reviews then took 141.6 seconds on that T4, one at a time, using 6.23 GB. The hosted model answered 300 few-shot requests in 30.4 seconds but needs a remote service and the much larger model. The fine-tuned model was 0.063 higher in balanced accuracy overall, but much of that sits in the star-word titles. The gain is modest and not firm.

**One decision I investigated with my agent.** In a trial run Phi-4-mini trained only 8,912,896 parameters. The agent showed this equals exactly 2 of the model's 4 kinds of layers, because Unsloth's layer pattern did not include its combined layer names, and a retest still trained only those two. It listed three options (keep the weaker adapter, work around Unsloth, switch models) and recommended Gemma 3 4B. I chose the switch.
